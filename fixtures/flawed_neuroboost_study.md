## Abstract

We examined whether NeuroBoost-X improves memory. Twelve adult mice received NeuroBoost-X in drinking water for seven days. The treated mice completed a maze 18% faster than the untreated mice. We therefore conclude that NeuroBoost-X causes durable memory improvement in people and should be offered to students before examinations.

## Methods

Mice were assigned to cages based on availability rather than randomization. The technician knew which cages received NeuroBoost-X. Maze completion time was measured once on day seven. We did not measure learning rate, anxiety, locomotor activity, sleep, dose intake, or adverse effects. No human participants were enrolled.

## Results

The six treated mice had a lower mean maze completion time than the six untreated mice. One treated mouse was excluded after testing because it remained still for most of the task. The analysis reports no confidence intervals, individual observations, pre-registration, or correction for exclusions.

In a separate pilot using the same cages, the treated and untreated mice had indistinguishable maze completion times. This null result was not included in the main calculation because the spreadsheet file was misplaced.
